Distributed solar homes · Concept design

A neighborhood energy network, designed around each home.

Connect homes with solar panels, smart meters, and batteries to measure local energy, share verified surplus, and settle community transactions with a proposed energy credit called InerCoin.

Explore the system Review the risks

01 · Business context

Why build this network?

A local energy community can make better use of rooftop generation: households with temporary surplus can support participating homes with demand, while storage helps shift energy over time.

For households

More value from local solar

Track production, consumption, and battery state; see when a home exports energy or draws from the community.

For the operator

One view of many homes

Monitor network health, meter data, settlement records, and exceptions through one service and operational dashboard.

For the community

Transparent accounting

Use auditable readings and published rules to calculate who supplied or consumed energy and how credits were assigned.

Important physical distinction: InerCoin does not move electricity. Power flows through the electrical network under the grid operator's rules. The token is only a proposed accounting or settlement instrument for measured, eligible energy.

02 · System architecture

Many independent homes, one service endpoint

Each home is a node with its own panels, meter, battery controller, and edge gateway. Nodes communicate securely with a central service. That service uses a replicated database cluster so a single database machine failure does not necessarily stop the system.

The homes are data-producing nodes, not database consensus members. Raft runs among stable server-side replicas; it is not an energy trading algorithm.

03 · E-C-A and message flow

Authenticate first, then process the event

E-C-A here means Event – Control – Authentication, using the terminology supplied for this project. For a secure implementation, authentication must happen at the transport or message boundary before an event is accepted or control logic runs.

Authenticate

Verify device identity, message signature, authorization, and session status.

Receive event

Accept a typed reading with home ID, device ID, time, sequence, value, and unit.

Validate & deduplicate

Check range, ownership, freshness, sequence, and idempotency before storage.

Control & calculate

Update derived energy state and issue only policy-authorized recommendations.

Record & settle

Commit audit data; create InerCoin entries only from eligible, reconciled energy.

Example telemetry event

{
  "home_id": "home-104",
  "device_id": "meter-104-battery",
  "event_id": "boot-7:8274",
  "timestamp": "2026-11-23T13:49:00Z",
  "sequence": 8274,
  "metric": "battery_soc",
  "value": 62.5,
  "unit": "percent"
}

Separate telemetry and commands

Meter readings are observations. Battery or inverter commands are high-impact actions and need a separate authorization path, strict limits, expiration, acknowledgement, and local safety enforcement.

The central service should not be able to override hardware protections or manufacturer limits.

04 · Proposed energy credit

InerCoin: a settlement concept, not a physical currency of electrons

InerCoin is hypothetical in this design. It could represent a community credit for verified net energy delivered, subject to local electricity-market, consumer-protection, tax, and financial rules. It should not be presented as a real or legally approved currency.

Illustrative settlement

Metered export becomes a proposed credit

  1. Collect interval readings from approved meters.
  2. Reconcile imports, exports, losses, and corrections.
  3. Apply a published tariff and eligibility policy.
  4. Post a signed, auditable debit/credit entry.
  5. Allow disputes and reversals without deleting history.
Keep the ledger honest

Separate the energy record from token custody

Keep raw meter readings and settlement calculations in the operational data platform. If a token ledger is later justified, use it as a separate settlement component with explicit governance.

A token balance is not proof that energy physically flowed, and a blockchain cannot make a compromised meter reading true.

Design questionRecommendation for a pilot
What is one InerCoin worth?Define a clear unit and conversion policy (for example, a credit per eligible kWh); publish whether it is redeemable, transferable, or only an internal accounting unit.
Who validates a transaction?Use the approved meter, reconciliation rules, and authorized operator workflow. Do not treat a token consensus vote as a substitute for metrology.
Who can reverse an error?Use append-only correction entries with actor, reason, and evidence; never erase the original record.
Is a public blockchain required?Not for a single operator. Start with a conventional auditable ledger; consider distributed-ledger technology only if independent parties need shared governance.

Business acceptance tests · proposed

Prove that energy and credits reconcile

These scenario tests define what a future tariff and settlement service should guarantee. They are proposals, not tests currently implemented in the Python prototype, which has no interval meter store, allocation engine, or InerCoin ledger.

TestExample and pass conditionBusiness limitation addressed
BX1 · Credit only verified export During one interval, PV produces 10 kWh, the home uses 7 kWh, the battery stores 2 kWh, and the certified export meter records 1 kWh. At most 1 kWh is eligible for an export credit—not 3 or 10 kWh. Prevents counting self-consumption or battery charging as energy sold to the community.
BX2 · Deterministic tariff and rounding Given an illustrative published rate of 0.08 InerCoin/kWh and 1.25 eligible kWh, calculate 0.10 InerCoin using a documented precision and rounding rule; repeated calculations produce the same result. Prevents inconsistent payouts, hidden rounding choices, and disputes over how the advertised tariff was applied. The rate is an example, not a proposed real-world price.
BX3 · Duplicate and late readings Submit the same interval event twice, then deliver a delayed reading after a network outage. The duplicate creates no second credit; the late interval remains pending until reconciliation, then settles once. Prevents duplicate credits and premature settlement when homes buffer data offline or messages arrive out of order.
BX4 · Limited community supply With 4 kWh available and eligible requests totalling 7 kWh, the allocation never exceeds 4 kWh and follows a published priority or pro-rata rule. Unserved demand is reported rather than silently promised. Prevents overselling energy and opaque or unfair allocation among participating homes.
BX5 · Missing or suspect meter interval If an export reading is missing, out of range, or fails meter reconciliation, mark settlement as pending/exception and issue no final credit until an approved correction or estimate policy is applied. Prevents paying against incomplete or untrusted data and makes operational exceptions visible.
BX6 · Battery reserve and eligible discharge A proposed discharge must not cross the household's configured reserve or manufacturer safety limits. If it would, reject the dispatch recommendation and do not count that energy as available supply. Protects household backup needs and battery life; avoids selling energy that cannot safely be delivered.
BX7 · Tariff changes are versioned Changing a tariff for a new settlement period does not silently alter already finalized periods. Every credit records the tariff version and interval used. Prevents retroactive price changes, improves auditability, and supports transparent customer statements.
BX8 · Dispute and correction trail A corrected meter value creates a linked reversal/adjustment with an actor, reason, and evidence; the original transaction remains visible and the final balance reconciles. Supports customer disputes and audit requirements without erasing history or hiding operator errors.
Prototype boundary: BX1–BX8 should become integration and acceptance tests only after meter intervals, tariff rules, allocation policy, and a settlement ledger are specified. The current unit tests validate sensor parsing, energy arithmetic, battery bounds, and alerts; they do not prove billing correctness or commercial fairness.

05 · Consistency and availability

Raft for the database, not for household energy control

Three replicas

A three-member Raft group can commit when a majority (two) is available and can tolerate one unavailable member, assuming correct deployment and independent failure domains.

One service address

Homes connect to one logical API/MQTT endpoint, backed by redundant service instances. This gives a simple client configuration without making one computer the only server.

Offline-tolerant homes

Gateways buffer signed, sequenced events during outages. Local inverter protections continue independently; delayed telemetry is reconciled when connectivity returns.

Availability caveat: Three database copies on one machine, one rack, one power feed, or one site do not provide meaningful infrastructure resilience. Replication is also not a backup; use tested, isolated backups and recovery drills.

06 · Security and design limitations

Threats to address before connecting real homes

The current Python project is a teaching prototype, not a production security boundary. Its tests demonstrate useful checks, but they do not provide device authentication, trusted measurements, or safe control of real batteries.

High Hashing IDs is not authentication

The prototype uses a public salt and short IDs such as S1. Anyone who can read the registry can test guesses. A hash lookup does not prove that the sender controls the sensor.

Improve: provision each device with a unique, random credential held in secure hardware where possible; use mutual TLS or signed messages, key rotation, revocation, and a secure enrollment process. Never use a shared fleet secret.

High Meter integrity and physical tampering

A valid device can still report a faulty, misconfigured, or physically manipulated measurement. Cryptographic signatures prove message origin and integrity, not that the physical reading is correct.

Improve: use certified meters, tamper evidence, calibration and anomaly checks, independent reconciliation, and clear dispute procedures. Treat meter trust as a separate problem from network security.

High Unsafe remote control

A compromised service or stolen operator credential could send battery commands. The demo calculates battery state but does not implement a production command authorization or hardware safety layer.

Improve: keep hard safety limits at the home controller; use least privilege, signed and expiring commands, operator approval for sensitive actions, rate limits, acknowledgements, and a safe offline mode.

High Replays, clocks, and restart behavior

The in-memory replay guard is lost on restart and relies on a timestamp supplied by the sender. A timestamp alone can be misleading if clocks drift or are changed.

Improve: persist sequence state, bind a monotonic counter to device identity and boot/session ID, enforce idempotency at storage, use trusted time where available, and define recovery for counter resets.

High Central service is a high-value target

A central service simplifies operations but creates a concentration of control, personal energy data, and settlement authority. A service outage or compromise can affect every connected home.

Improve: isolate public ingress, use network segmentation, hardened deployment, short-lived credentials, MFA for operators, auditable privileged access, tested incident response, and separate production from development.

Medium Replication, consensus, and backups

Raft provides consistency among database members; it does not prevent an authorized but malicious application from writing false data, and it does not protect against a quorum loss or correlated site failure.

Improve: use independent failure zones, encrypted and access-controlled backups, point-in-time recovery, restore testing, monitoring, and an explicit degraded-mode policy.

Medium Privacy and household inference

High-frequency consumption data may reveal occupancy and routines. Long retention and broad access increase privacy and breach impact.

Improve: minimize collection, aggregate where possible, define retention and deletion policies, encrypt data in transit and at rest, and enforce tenant-level access and audit trails.

Medium InerCoin, fraud, and regulation

A token creates additional risks: double settlement, meter collusion, stolen keys, irreversible transfers, contract bugs, price volatility, and legal classification as a payment or financial product.

Improve: pilot non-transferable credits first, reconcile against trusted meter intervals, define dispute and reversal processes, commission legal review, and do not deploy smart contracts before threat modeling and independent audits.

Runnable regression tests

New input-validation cases

The Python suite adds parameterized tests for security edge cases that the prototype can enforce today:

  • X7: reject boolean, string, negative, NaN, or infinite timestamps.
  • X8: reject NaN and positive or negative infinity for production, consumption, and battery measurements.
  • X9: reject negative and non-finite timestamps in the replay guard.
  • X10: reject NaN in battery balance, duration, and capacity calculations.

Run them from smart-energy with python -m pytest -v tests/test_security.py.

Future tests before a pilot

Tests that need production components

  • Unknown, revoked, or expired device credentials and invalid message signatures are denied.
  • Duplicate event IDs do not create duplicate readings or InerCoin credits, including after a service restart.
  • A device cannot read another home's data or issue a command for another home.
  • Expired, modified, unauthorized, or replayed battery commands are rejected by the home controller.
  • Database quorum loss, delayed telemetry, and recovery do not create false successful settlements.

These are design requirements, not implemented tests: the current prototype has no credentials, command service, durable event store, or token ledger.

07 · Delivery plan

Build trust before adding token complexity

Phase 1 · Safe telemetry pilot

  • Choose certified meters and a small set of test homes.
  • Implement device enrollment, mutual authentication, and TLS.
  • Add durable event IDs, schema validation, buffering, and audit records.
  • Run threat modeling and tabletop outage/incident exercises.

Phase 2 · Reconciliation and settlement

  • Define import/export intervals, losses, tariff, and dispute rules.
  • Test reconciliation and corrections with simulated and real meter data.
  • Assess legal, privacy, tax, and consumer obligations.
  • Trial InerCoin as an internal credit only if it solves a real business need.
Success measures: meter-data completeness and accuracy, time to detect a failed node, recovery time after an outage, settlement mismatch rate, unresolved disputes, and verified security-control coverage.
